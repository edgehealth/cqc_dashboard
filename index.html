What CQC’s local authority assessments show about good adult social care

CQC has now assessed how almost every council in England meets its Care Act duties. This dashboard brings the 152 published reports together, so councils can see how they compare, what higher-scoring councils do differently, and who to learn from.

CQC scores each council from 1 to 4 on 9 quality statements: 1 means significant shortfalls, 2 some shortfalls, 3 a good standard and 4 an exceptional standard. The findings are CQC’s, quoted from its published reports. This site is intended to support learning between councils, not to rank them. Data as at 19 November 2026.

Main findings

Assessing needs

Higher-scoring councils prioritised people on waiting lists by risk

In all 8 councils that scored 1 (significant shortfalls), CQC described people waiting whom no one was reliably checking on. In all 31 councils that scored 3 or 4 (good or exceptional) and had a waiting list, CQC described people being prioritised by risk.

Leadership

Councils that scored well on governance tended to score well elsewhere

Councils scoring 1 (significant shortfalls) on governance averaged {{gov.one}} across the other eight quality statements. Those scoring 3 averaged {{gov.three}}, and those scoring 4 (exceptional) averaged {{gov.four}}. This shows an association, not cause and effect, and governance assessments draw partly on the same evidence.

Outstanding councils

What the four outstanding councils had in common

Barking and Dagenham, Camden, Kensington and Chelsea and Wigan all scored 4 on partnerships and communities and on governance. Wigan was the only council to score 4 on assessing needs.
