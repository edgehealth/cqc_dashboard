How this analysis was done

This dashboard is Edge Health’s independent analysis of published CQC local authority assessment reports. It is not an official CQC or government publication.

Coverage

  1. The analysis uses all 152 local authority assessment reports CQC had published, from May 2024 to November 2026. Rutland is the only council whose report has not yet been published.
  2. The councils include 4 rated outstanding (Barking and Dagenham, Camden, Kensington and Chelsea, and Wigan), 91 rated good, 54 rated requires improvement and 3 rated inadequate (Blackpool, East Riding of Yorkshire, and Salford).
  3. Where CQC has replaced a report with a newer one, the earlier report has been used and flagged. This applies to Bath and North East Somerset and Wirral.
  4. All figures on this site, including the ratings split and averages, are calculated from these 152 reports. Data is as at 19 November 2026.

Scores

  1. Each report scores 9 quality statements from 1 to 4. CQC defines these as: 1, evidence shows significant shortfalls; 2, evidence shows some shortfalls; 3, evidence shows a good standard; 4, evidence shows an exceptional standard.
  2. Scores were taken from the report text.
  3. The overall percentage appears only in a chart near the start of each report, so it was read from there. In every report it matched the average of the 9 scores, as a percentage, rounded up.
  4. Scores are categories rather than measurements. CQC itself combines the 9 scores into an overall percentage, and averages are used in the same way here, as a guide.
  5. Ratings follow the bands shown in each report: inadequate up to 38%, requires improvement 39% to 62%, good 63% to 87% and outstanding 88% and above.

Findings and examples

  1. For each quality statement, the relevant section of the reports was reviewed for the councils that scored lowest and highest, and for a selection of councils in between, to compare what CQC found.
  2. Examples of practice come from one source: what CQC said councils did well, in CQC’s own words, with the page number. Only councils that scored 3 or 4 on a statement are used as examples for it.
  3. Some examples were selected and tagged by Edge Health analysts. The rest were selected automatically from each report, favouring sentences with specific detail and leaving out general wording that CQC uses in many reports.
  4. The same examples appear on the quality statement pages, as strengths on each council’s profile, and as suggestions for other councils.
  5. For assessing needs, the main pattern was checked across every council that scored 1, 3 or 4.

Council profiles

  1. Statements scoring 1 or 2 are shown as areas to improve, with sentences from the council’s report describing CQC’s concerns, examples from councils that scored higher, and councils in the same region to talk to.
  2. Statements scoring 3 or 4 are shown as strengths, with what CQC said the council did well and councils in the same region that scored lower.
  3. Sentences from CQC’s reports include both CQC’s own judgements and views CQC reported from people, staff and partners. CQC’s judgements are shown first where possible.
  4. Councils are compared with others in the same region because regions are simple and widely understood. Statistical neighbour groups, such as CIPFA nearest neighbours, may be a closer comparison and could be added.

Use of AI

  1. AI tools were used to extract scores from the 152 reports, to select sentences from each report describing what CQC found, and to help draft the summaries on this site.
  2. The rules for what to select were reviewed and refined over several rounds.
  3. A sample of the findings and examples was checked manually against the original reports.
  4. All sentences shown as CQC’s findings are quoted from the reports, with page numbers, so you can check them against the source.

Limitations

  1. A pattern shows what tends to go together, not what causes a score.
  2. Councils differ in size, funding and local demand. Very small councils, such as the City of London and the Isles of Scilly, may not compare directly with large ones.
  3. Reports describe each council at the time of its assessment, and CQC’s approach developed over the programme. Some councils will have changed since.
  4. Findings selected automatically may occasionally be out of context. Each one gives the report page so you can check CQC’s full wording.
